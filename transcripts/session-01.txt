  Q4. When the total doesn't divide evenly, who absorbs the leftover rupees?

  Take a 1000 PKR bill split 3 ways, which is 333.33 each:

  - A) Everyone else rounds up, payer takes the remainder. Friends each send
    334, and the payer's share is 332. Everyone else gets one simple number, the
    sum is exactly 1000, and the payer (who did the work) gets the small
    benefit.
  - B) Uneven shares. Two people send 333 and one sends 334. The sum is exact,
    but the message gets awkward ("Ali 334, others 333"), and who is "unlucky"
    is arbitrary.
  - C) Everyone else rounds down, payer covers the gap. Friends send 333 and the
    payer's share is 334. The sum is exact, and the payer quietly loses a rupee
    or two.

  My recommendation: A. One number for everyone fits a single WhatsApp message
  ("send 334 each to …"), the total always adds up exactly, and it's easy to
  write acceptance criteria for.

  One edge case this exposes, which needs a rule. With tiny totals and many
  people, rounding up can push the payer's share negative. For example, 5 PKR
  split among 10 people: 9 friends × 1 = 9 > 5. I recommend a minimum-total
  rule: the total must be at least the number of people (1 PKR each), otherwise
  show "Total must be at least 1 PKR per person." That rule guarantees the
  payer's share is never negative and no friend owes 0.

  Hmm, let me double-check that claim before you rely on it. With total = N
  exactly, others pay ceil(1) = 1, and the payer pays N − (N−1) = 1. That works.
  But with total = 11 and N = 10, others pay ceil(1.1) = 2, so 9 × 2 = 18 > 11,
  and the payer's share is −7. So "at least 1 per person" is NOT enough. The
  rule that actually guarantees a non-negative payer share under option A is:

  ▎ (N − 1) × ceil(total / N) ≤ total

  Realistic bills (hundreds or thousands of PKR, under about 20 people) never
  come close to this. But the reviewer will try odd inputs, so we'd show a clear
  error such as "Total is too small to split between N people in whole rupees."

  That's a slightly awkward rule to explain, which is an argument for C. Under
  C, friends round down and the payer covers the leftover, so the payer's share
  is never negative. The only rule needed is "total ≥ number of people," so
  nobody owes 0.

  Revised recommendation: C, because the edge-case rule is simpler and there are
  no surprises. The payer losing at most N−1 rupees is negligible. A remains a
  valid choice if you prefer the payer to benefit, at the cost of that awkward
  guard.

  A or C?

⏺ That's the whole design tree. Here's everything we agreed on in one place, so
  you can check it before we build.

  Product

  - Who it's for: only the person who paid the bill (the Bill Payer). Group
    Members never open the app; they just get a WhatsApp message.
  - How the bill is split: an equal split in PKR, whole rupees only.
  - Rounding: each friend's share rounds up and the payer's own share takes the
    remainder. For example, 1000 split 3 ways is 334, 334 and 332.
    - If the total is too small for that to work, the app shows "Total is too
      small to split between N people in whole rupees."
  - Money: always stored as whole-number rupees, never floats. Shown with
    thousands separators ("1,000 PKR").

  What the app accepts

  - Total:
    - Whole rupees, more than 0 and at most 10,000,000.
    - Commas and spaces are stripped.
    - Decimals, letters and 1e5 are rejected with a clear message.
    - The field is a text input set to numbers (inputmode="numeric"), not a
      number input.
  - People:
    - 2 to 50 people in total.
    - Names are 1–30 characters after trimming, and any language or emoji is
      fine.
    - The payer's name is required.
    - Duplicates are rejected regardless of capital letters, and that includes
      the payer's name.
  - Payment details: one optional free-text box of up to 200 characters. If it's
    empty, that line is left out of the message.

  Tracking

  - Marking payments: each friend gets a paid tick that can be undone. The payer
    is automatically marked as "paid (you)".
  - Summary: shows "X of N paid · Y PKR still to collect", or "Everyone has paid
    ✓".
  - Editing: any change that alters the shares asks for confirmation, then
    clears the ticks. Renaming someone or editing the payment details keeps the
    ticks.
  - Saving: one bill is saved in the browser on that device only. "New bill"
    asks for confirmation on the page itself (no browser popup).
  - Message:
    - It's always shown on screen.
    - It has a Copy button (showing "Copied ✓", or a fallback message if copying
      is blocked) and an Open in WhatsApp button.
    - It doesn't list names.
  - Left out: reminder messages, screenshot uploads, partial payments, and
    keeping several bills at once.

  Tech and delivery

  - Stack: Vite + React + TypeScript + plain CSS + Vitest.
  - Where the maths lives: a pure split.ts, with a test checking that the shares
    always add up to the total.
  - Layout: follows the brief (README.md, src/, docs/, transcripts/).
  - Sharing: a public GitHub repo, with Vercel as an optional public URL.
  - Visual design: deferred to your next session. This session covers behaviour,
    states and accessibility basics only.
